# Roam — Voice & Tone Guide

*Adapted from the editorial philosophy behind Every (every.to), translated for a
retention marketing studio. This is a living document: it should be revised
every time a sentence that sounds wrong gets published, and the revision should
say why.*

---

## The one-line test

Every sentence on the site, in a deck, or in a client email should sound like
**a sharp colleague explaining something real over coffee** — never like a
vendor, never like a professor, never like a LinkedIn post.

---

## Core tenets

### 1. Accessible intellectualism
We work in a genuinely technical field — cohort math, LTV curves, churn-risk
scoring, incrementality. The intelligence shows in how *plainly* we can say it,
not how densely.

- Say: "Most brands lose 7 of every 10 first-time buyers, forever."
- Never: "Industry benchmarks indicate suboptimal second-purchase conversion."
- Rule: if a founder can't repeat the sentence to their cofounder from memory,
  rewrite it.

### 2. Optimistic realism
Retention compounds, and that's genuinely exciting — but it is slow, it is
unglamorous, and nothing about it is a silver bullet. We never promise magic.
We show the math and let it be impressive on its own.

- Say: "This takes 90 days to show up, and then it never stops showing up."
- Never: "10x your revenue with one flow."
- AI note (borrowed straight from Every): we use AI heavily in production and
  say so plainly. It is a power tool, not a magician. Human judgment signs
  every send.

### 3. Lived experience
We write from inside the inboxes and accounts we run — receipts, not
observations. First person is welcome. Specifics beat categories.

- Say: "We watched a win-back flow pay for our entire retainer in eleven days.
  Here's the send that did it."
- Never: "Studies show win-back campaigns can be effective."

### 4. Intellectual generosity
We pose real questions and publish the playbook. No gatekeeping, no dogma,
no trend-chasing. Confidence looks like showing your work.

- Say: "What does a customer owe you? Nothing. Start there."
- Never: "The 7 secrets top brands don't want you to know."

## Openings (the three hooks)

Stolen with pride from Every's structure guidance. Every essay, case study,
and even section of the site should open with one of these:

1. **The already-happened.** A past-tense revelation.
   *"Three weeks ago, your best customer bought from you for the last time."*
2. **The paradigm flip.** One sentence that reverses an assumption.
   *"Your welcome flow is a first date. Stop proposing."*
3. **The visceral moment.** Sensory detail plus emotion.
   *"The unsubscribe doesn't sting. The silence does."*

If an opening is a definition, a greeting, or a mission statement — cut it and
start at the second paragraph.

---

## On philosophers and big ideas

We think in public and we're allowed to argue with dead Greeks — **in prose,
in essays, in titles** ("Heraclitus was wrong about your customers").
We do not put quotes from authorities on the walls of the site. The site
demonstrates thinking; it doesn't cite it. Borrowed authority is the opposite
of accessible intellectualism.

---

## Language rules

- Banned: *omnichannel, lifecycle solutions, leverage (as a verb), unlock,
  supercharge, seamless, best-in-class, secret sauce, growth hack.*
- Numbers always carry context: "+38% repeat purchase rate" not "+38%."
- Controls and CTAs say exactly what happens: "Start a project," not
  "Let's talk possibilities."
- Contractions on. Second person welcome. Em-dashes in moderation.
- Humor is dry and earned, never wacky.

---

## The AI-native workflow (internal)

Following Every's practice: maintain a Claude Project loaded with this guide,
our published field notes, and our best client emails. Use it to draft in our
voice, then edit by hand. Feed it our past writing so it learns our rhythms —
and our tics, so it can flag them. AI drafts, humans decide. Clients and
sponsors never touch editorial judgment.
